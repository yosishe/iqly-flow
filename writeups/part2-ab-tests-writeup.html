Three experiments — decisions before results

Part 2 of 3 — A/B Tests · Three full standalone variants of the submitted control

Decision. Test value revealed, effort required and signup timing. The generated differences are configuration plus truthful copy.

Metric. Proposed production primary: verified accounts within seven days / eligible exposed new visitors. Brief-literal accounts/page visits and prototype session conversion remain separate.

Method. Prospective tests only: visitor ITT, fixed horizon, mature cohorts and 95% risk-ratio intervals. Fast completion is diagnostic, not an exclusion.

Test 1 — Result gating · result → signup · variant-1-score-first.html

Hypothesis: showing the range and rank before signup may improve trust and starts but weaken the account ask; the net effect is unknown. Change: score-first reveals the illustrative range/rank free; the account adds review and local history. The landing promise follows the offer truthfully. KPIs: verified accounts per eligible visitor; starts, completion, gate entry (signup view/result teaser) and form conversion separately. Rule: ship if the variant/control primary ratio's lower 95% bound exceeds 0.90 and quality guardrails hold. This 10% tolerance is a product policy for a hypothesized trust benefit. A superiority-only rule may be selected and sized before enrollment, never after seeing data. Risk: more free content also lengthens the result screen and changes the account payload; the test cannot isolate trust from layout. An inconclusive interval keeps the control.

Test 2 — Quiz length · quiz start and completion · variant-2-eight-questions.html

Hypothesis: eight questions reduce effort enough to improve end-to-end verified conversion despite a potentially less convincing result. Change: eight items from the same bank, two per area, covering the three intended difficulty tiers; count and estimated duration copy change accordingly. Composition, milestone positions and score resolution change too. The existing top-band rule admits at most one wrong answer at both lengths. KPIs: verified accounts per eligible visitor; completion, duration, per-item loss and gate entry; inspect band shifts, verification and retakes. Rule: primary ratio lower 95% bound >1.00 and gate-entry ratio lower bound >0.95. An inconclusive primary or guardrail keeps twelve. A +20% true lift is a sizing illustration, not a minimum shipping threshold. Risk: crediting length for a composition effect. A production test needs new count-neutral acquisition copy; the submitted creatives promote the twelve-question control.

Test 3 — Signup timing · entry · variant-3-signup-first.html

Hypothesis: asking before question one may capture accounts before quiz attrition while reducing verification or result reach. Change: the same free account precedes the quiz; landing and signup copy disclose that timing. The result opens directly after completion. KPIs: verified accounts per eligible visitor, counting accounts without a quiz start; quiz starts/account, full results/account and full results/eligible visitor. Rule: primary ratio lower 95% bound >1.05 and full-result/account ratio lower bound ≥1.00. This strict zero-loss quality policy may remain inconclusive even when true quality is equal; any tolerated-loss margin requires a separate decision before enrollment. Risk: an earlier numerator can look like growth without delivered value, and conditional account groups differ between arms. Keep control when evidence is insufficient. Test after the gating decision with a newly verified control; an after-question-three ask is a credible later alternative.

Alternatives and AI

Question-first entry, a coarse free band and disclosure prominence remain useful alternatives. An earlier fold check exposed a layout tradeoff in one question-first design, not a general prohibition.
