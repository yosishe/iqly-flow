Three creatives — one recognizable promise

Part 3 of 3 — Ad Creatives · 320×50 · 250×250 · 1080×1920

Concept. A real sample task and a specific free-test promise, carried through to the landing and question one.

Objective. Useful accounts per impression and acquisition cost; clicks and post-click account conversion are separate diagnostic terms.

Tradeoff. Clear payment/account wording may affect clicks and qualified completion in either direction; no CTR or conversion effect has been measured.

320 × 50 — mobile banner

Hook: “Free IQ test”, “12 questions · ~5 min · no payment”, and “Start”. At this small size I use a compact text promise rather than shrink the puzzle beyond useful recognition. Continuity: the landing repeats the duration/count and explains the free account boundary before the test starts. The banner does not promise “no signup” or a free precise score. Tradeoff: it has less room to explain the exchange than the vertical. This may broaden or weaken qualification; the effect is untested. Measurement: accounts per impression and cost per verified account, with CTR, post-click conversion and accidental-click diagnostics alongside. Both 320×50 and optional 640×100 exports are supplied; the latter represents the same banner at 2× pixel density.

250 × 250 — square

Hook: “Which tile completes the grid?” uses the actual first question, explicitly labelled “Sample · Q1 of 12”. The options are not drawn as interactive buttons. Continuity: the user sees the same task in the control, and its explanation is available on the gate screen. The footer visibly includes “Free IQ test · 12 questions · ~5 min · no payment”. Tradeoff: small text and a recognizable sample leave little room for extra claims; showing Q1 also gives some visitors prior exposure, so acquisition must be held constant in a test. Measurement: accounts per impression and post-click completion, not puzzle clicks alone. The final PNG was inspected at its exact 250×250 dimensions.

1080 × 1920 — full-screen vertical

Hook: the same sample, 12 / ~5 min / 4 areas, “Start the test”, and the full boundary: “Free, no payment: all 12 questions and how many you got right. Free account (email only, no password): IQ range and rank. No payment, ever. Not a clinical IQ assessment.” Continuity: these words describe the control's exchange. Tradeoff: the copy occupies attention that could go to the puzzle; whether it qualifies clicks better is a hypothesis. All existing content now fits the declared rectangle, x=72..1008 and y=270..1250. These are internal design margins; each real placement still needs its own overlay check. Measurement: verified accounts per impression with placement quality and cost, then a separately planned disclosure comparison if needed; no CTR loss is presumed.

Rejected and attributed

No invented certification, exact IQ result, “Only 1%”, countdown, viewer diagnosis or “no signup” promise. The original concept and selection are documented as user decisions; Claude Code created the artwork and exports and found the earlier square-footer issue. The final Codex audit preserved that already-correct square wording, corrected vertical bounds and re-exported all sizes. Historical renderer metadata is incomplete; current exports and HTML are checked together.

All creatives promote the submitted control. A length/timing experiment needs acquisition copy compatible with both arms. No placement approval or commercial performance is claimed.
